Shopping notes

Your CSSBuy spreadsheet needs more than a product-price column

2026-10-23 · 3 min read

Keep known costs, missing charges and dated estimates separate so a rough budget stays readable.

A shortlist can look comfortably within budget until shipping enters the picture. The problem is often the spreadsheet: a product-price column has quietly become a total-cost column. Splitting those ideas early makes a CSSBuy spreadsheet much more useful.

Keep the starting price attached to its source

Record the selected option, currency and date checked beside the listing price. If the seller page changes, you will know which part of your budget needs another look. Avoid mixing a converted estimate with an original-currency figure under the same heading.

A conversion can help you plan, but label its date and assumptions. It is still an estimate until the actual payment details are known.

Leave unknown charges visibly unknown

Use separate fields for domestic shipping, service charges where applicable, and international parcel costs. An empty field should mean “not checked,” not zero. A note such as “quote pending” is easier to understand than a total that silently skips a charge.

The comparison CSV linked from the main guide can be a starting point. Add a status column if that helps distinguish a listing price from a quote and an actual paid amount.

Compare parcel quotes on the same basis

When quotes become available, save the date, currency, parcel weight and dimensions used. Check the included services and restrictions. Two headline figures are not a fair comparison if they describe different packing assumptions or different included charges.

If you change the parcel contents, mark the earlier estimate as old rather than carrying it forward unchanged. The quote belongs to the parcel details used to obtain it.

Keep a little room for the decision

You do not need to turn a shopping shortlist into an accounting system. The aim is to see what is known, what is missing and which choice changes the budget. Keep an estimated total separate from confirmed paid costs, and avoid promising yourself a final figure too early.
